Steps

A-01

Through-Glass Via Forming

TGV Laser

LPKF · Philoptics

A laser marks each via in the glass panel and an etch opens the holes.

Materials  Glass Core Panels

Next inspection  Metrology (via diameter, taper) · AOI (cracks, chipping)

A-02

Glass Via Metallization

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

A seed layer goes onto the glass, since plated copper does not grip glass on its own, and the vias are filled with copper.

Materials  —Consumables  Core Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  Cross-section (via fill) · Lab test (adhesion)

A-03

Carrier Preparation

Carrier Panel

Prepared in-house by the substrate maker

A rigid carrier panel is clad with a peelable ultra-thin copper foil, the base the first layer is built on.

Materials  Ultra-Thin Carrier Foil

Next inspection  AOI (foil surface)

A-04

Core Laminate

Vacuum Hot Press

Kitagawa Seiki · Burkle · Lauffer · Cedal

The laminate maker presses glass-cloth prepreg and copper foil into the substrate core, T-glass with BT or low-CTE epoxy where a large die must stay flat.

Materials  Substrate Core Laminate · BT Laminate

Next inspection  Metrology (thickness, CTE, flatness)

A-05

Core Drilling

Drilling Machine Laser Via Drill

Via Mechanics · Schmoll · Tongtai · Mitsubishi Electric · ESI

Through-holes are drilled through the core, by mechanical drill or by CO2 laser on thin cores.

Materials  —

Next inspection  X-ray (hole position)

A-06

Core Desmear

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

Resin smeared by the drill is cleaned off the hole walls so the copper plates onto a clean surface.

Materials  —Consumables  Core Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  Cross-section (hole wall)

A-07

Core Electroless Copper

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

A thin electroless copper layer is deposited on the hole walls, since electroplating needs a conductive surface to start.

Materials  —Consumables  Core Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  Cross-section (backlight, seed coverage)

A-08

Through-Hole Plating

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

Electrolytic copper thickens the hole walls and the core faces, joining the two sides of the core.

Materials  —Consumables  Core Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  Cross-section (hole plating) · Metrology (copper thickness)

A-09

Hole Plugging

Plugging Machine

Done in-house by the substrate maker

The plated holes are filled with resin so the layers pressed above them lie flat.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Cross-section (voids in the plug)

A-10

Plug Grinding

Brush Grinder

Done in-house by the substrate maker

The cured plug resin standing above the surface is ground flat with the copper.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (plug flatness)

A-11

Core Dry Film

Wet-Process Line

Tokyo Kakoki · Schmid · Chemcut · Kuttler

The core copper is cleaned and a dry-film resist laminated on for the core circuits.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (resist pattern), after developing

A-12

Core Exposure

Substrate LDI

Orbotech · ORC · Screen · Adtec · Ushio

Direct imaging writes the core circuit pattern into the dry film.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (resist pattern), after developing

A-13

Core Develop, Etch and Strip

Wet-Process Line

Tokyo Kakoki · Schmid · Chemcut · Kuttler

The unexposed film is washed off, the bare copper etched away and the film stripped, leaving the core circuits.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (core pattern)

A-14

Core AOI

AOI

Orbotech · Utechzone · Machvision

The core circuits are scanned for opens and shorts before the first layer covers them.

Materials  —

Inspection  This step is the check: AOI (opens, shorts, nicks)

A-15

Copper Roughening

Wet-Process Line

Tokyo Kakoki · Schmid · Chemcut · Kuttler

The copper is micro-etched rough so the next film or prepreg grips it.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (roughness)

A-16

Build-Up Film Lamination

Vacuum Laminator

Meiki · Nikko-Materials

A sheet of ABF is vacuum-laminated onto each face, flowing into the gaps between the circuits below.

Materials  Build-Up Film

Next inspection  AOI (voids, wrinkles) · Metrology (film thickness)

A-17

Film Cure

Curing Oven

Done in-house by the substrate maker

The film is cured in an oven, once per layer and ten or more times a side on a large GPU substrate.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (warpage)

A-18

Prepreg and Foil Lamination

Lamination Press

Burkle · Lauffer · Cedal · Kitagawa Seiki

BT prepreg and ultra-thin copper foil are vacuum-pressed onto both faces and cured, once per layer.

Materials  BT Prepreg · Ultra-Thin Carrier Foil

Next inspection  X-ray (registration) · Cross-section (voids)

A-19

Laser Via Drilling

Laser Via Drill

Mitsubishi Electric · Via Mechanics · ESI · LPKF

A laser drills the vias through the new ABF film or BT prepreg down to the copper below, once per layer.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (via diameter, bottom residue)

A-20

Via Desmear

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

Resin left at the via bottoms is cleaned out and the dielectric surface roughened so the seed copper grips it.

Materials  —Consumables  SAP Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  AOI (via bottom residue)

A-21

Electroless Copper Seed

Plating Line

Atotech · Schmid · Chemcut · Manz

A thin electroless copper seed is deposited over the dielectric and into the vias, the base the traces are plated up from.

Materials  —Consumables  SAP Plating Chemistry

Next inspection  Cross-section (seed coverage)

A-22

Dry Film Lamination

Vacuum Laminator

Next inspection  AOI (resist pattern), after developing

A-23

Exposure

Substrate LDI

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (resist pattern) · Metrology (registration)

A-24

Develop

Wet-Process Line

A-25

Via Fill and Pattern Plating

Plating Line

Wet-Process Line

Next inspection  AOI (resist residue)

A-27

Flash Etch

Wet-Process Line

A quick etch removes the thin copper between the traces without eating the traces themselves.

Done in-house by the substrate maker

PART B

CCL

One card per step from glass fibre to finished laminate, and the equipment behind it.

Steps

B-01

Glass Fibre Drawing

Furnace Bushing

Heraeus · Tanaka Kikinzoku · Johnson Matthey

Glass is melted in a furnace and drawn through platinum-rhodium bushings into fine filaments twisted into yarn, while quartz yarn is drawn from fused-silica rods instead.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (filament diameter) · Lab test (yarn weight, strength)

B-02

Weaving

Loom

Toyota Industries · Tsudakoma · Picanol · Dornier

The yarn is warped onto beams and woven on air-jet looms into thin electronic cloth, in styles from thick 7628 down to ultra-thin 1017.

Materials  E-Glass Yarn (Standard) · Low-Dk Glass Yarn · Q-Glass Yarn (Low-Dk) · T-Glass Yarn (Low CTE)

Next inspection  AOI (weave defects) · Metrology (cloth weight, thickness)

B-03

Cloth Finishing

Finishing Line

Run in-house by the cloth maker

The cloth is heat-cleaned to burn off the weaving size, then coated with silane so the resin can bond to the glass.

Materials  —Consumables  Silane Coupling Agent

Next inspection  AOI (cloth defects) · Metrology (weight, thickness)

B-04

Foil Electrodeposition

Foil Line

Mifune · New Long · Nishi Industry

Copper dissolved in sulphuric acid is plated onto a rotating titanium drum and peeled off as raw foil, while rolled-annealed foil for flex is rolled from ingot instead.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (thickness) · AOI (pinholes)

B-05

Foil Surface Treatment

Foil Line

Mifune · New Long · Nishi Industry

The raw foil is roughened with copper nodules, kept very low on HVLP grades, then given barrier, anti-tarnish and silane coatings and slit into rolls.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (roughness, thickness) · Lab test (peel strength)

B-06

Varnish Compounding

Mixing Tank

Mixed in-house by the CCL maker

Resin, hardener, filler, flame retardant and solvent are mixed into varnish to a recipe per grade, the CCL maker’s core know-how.

Materials  Base Resin · Hardeners and Modifiers · Spherical Silica · Flame Retardants

Next inspection  Lab test (viscosity, gel time, solids)

B-07

Impregnation

Treater

Asia Metal · C.A. Litzler · Hirano Tecseed

Glass cloth runs through the varnish bath and metering rollers, which set how much resin it carries.

Materials  Q-Glass Cloth (Low-Dk) · Low-Dk Glass Cloth · E-Glass Cloth (Standard) · T-Glass Cloth (Low CTE)

Next inspection  Metrology (coated weight)

B-08

B-Stage Drying

Drying Oven

Asia Metal · C.A. Litzler

The tower oven drives off the solvent and half-cures the resin, turning the wet cloth into prepreg.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Lab test (resin content, gel time, resin flow, volatiles)

B-09

Prepreg Cutting

Lay-Up Line

Burkle · Kitagawa Seiki · Cedal

The prepreg is cut into sheets, some for the CCL press and some sold to board makers as bonding sheets.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (dry spots, foreign matter)

B-10

Lay-Up

Lay-Up Line

Burkle · Kitagawa Seiki · Cedal

Prepreg sheets are stacked between copper foils and steel separator plates into books, in a clean room.

Materials  HVLP Foil · Standard ED and RTF Foil · Rolled-Annealed Foil · Ultra-Thin Carrier Foil

Next inspection  AOI (foreign matter, wrinkles)

B-11

Press and Cure

Vacuum Hot Press

Kitagawa Seiki · Burkle · Lauffer · Cedal

The books are vacuum-pressed under heat until the resin fully cures into laminate.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (thickness)

B-12

Breakdown

Breakdown Line

Run in-house by the CCL maker

The pressed books are taken apart and the laminates separated from the steel plates, which are cleaned and reused.

Materials  —

Next inspection  AOI (dents, pits, scratches)

B-13

Trimming and Cutting

Panel Cutter

Run in-house by the CCL maker

The laminate edges are trimmed and the sheets cut to the panel sizes the board makers order.

Materials  —

Next inspection  Metrology (panel size, squareness)

B-14
