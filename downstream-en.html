PART A

Orientation

A1

How Demand Reaches the Chip

Every chip is ordered on behalf of an end product. Each layer orders ahead of its own sales and holds inventory, so a small change in end demand becomes a larger swing in chip orders and wafer starts; the upstream layers are mapped in the Equipment and Materials pages.

Where Chip Revenue Goes

PCs, computers and AI took 45.5% of chip demand in 2025, up from 34.9% in 2024, and every other end use lost share. Memory is set to overtake logic as the largest product in 2026, and the 2026 market forecast was raised in Aug 2026 to US$1,655bn, up 108%.

A5

The 2026 Memory Shock

Memory makers issued DDR4 end-of-life notices in mid-2025 and gave HBM and AI servers priority for capacity, so conventional DRAM contract prices roughly doubled in 1Q26. Products sold at fixed price points absorb the cost through higher prices, lower specifications or lower volumes.

PART B

Data Centre and AI Servers

B1

Milestones by Company

NVIDIA moved to a new rack generation every year, AMD and Huawei followed with rival rack-scale systems, and each cloud provider has its own accelerator ramping into 2027 and 2028.

B2

Market and Guidance

The data centre is now the largest end market for chips: server revenue rose 52% to US$166.3bn in 2Q26. Chip vendors guided next-quarter revenue above their prior-quarter outlooks, Amazon and Google raised 2026 capex guidance and Meta narrowed its range; Alchip gives no figure, and Microsoft restated its capex on a new lease basis. Each system ties its chips together with a scale-up network so they work as one machine: NVIDIA and AMD fit this in a single rack, while Google and Huawei link thousands of chips across more than 100 racks. PART D

Personal Computers

Milestones by Company

Windows 10 support ended in Oct 2025, PC brands raised prices as memory costs climbed, and Lenovo, HP, Dell and ASUS announced Arm laptops on NVIDIA’s RTX Spark for late 2026.

PART E

Automotive

E1

Milestones by Company

Carmakers turned to NVIDIA and in-house chips for assisted driving, Tesla started robotaxi service and Cybercab production, and China’s Nexperia export ban halted Honda’s Mexico plant. US tariffs and the end of the federal EV credit slowed US electric cars, while China halved the NEV purchase-tax break from Jan 2026.

E2

Electric Cars

Light-vehicle output rose 3.7% in 2025, but automotive chip revenue grew faster, up 6.4% to US$74.4bn by TechInsights’ count, as electric cars, more than 20m sold, carry more silicon. China leads adoption, and its NEV share of retail sales reached 65.2% in Aug 2026.

E3

Chip Content by Domain

Chip content is about US$1,400 in a battery-electric car against about US$750 in a combustion car, rising to about US$1,600 on average by 2030 and up to about US$2,500 at the high end; electrification and assisted driving add most of the gap.

PART F

Industrial, Consumer and Communications

F1

Sense, Decide, Act

Industrial, appliance and automotive systems run on the same loop: a sensor measures the physical world, an analog chip amplifies and digitises the signal, an MCU decides, and a discrete power device switches the motor. In an air conditioner, the loop turns a rise in room temperature into compressor action.

F2

Industrial Chip Vendors

Infineon, ST, Renesas and Rohm make all four chips in the loop, while specialists such as Sony, Bosch and Mitsubishi Electric focus on one or two. In 2Q26, industrial sales rose 30–53% YoY at ADI, Renesas, NXP, ST and TI, and 9% in Infineon’s industrial power segment.
